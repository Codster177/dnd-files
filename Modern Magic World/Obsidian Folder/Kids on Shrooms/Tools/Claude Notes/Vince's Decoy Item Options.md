Ruling to make: what exactly was the magic item [[Vincenzo (Vince) Melville|Vince]] hid in [[Sadie Marsh and Erin Kowalski]]'s apartment as his lightning rod. His note and the couple's note currently use a placeholder (a Neridoran prayer lamp that burns without oil); once an item is ruled, update both notes and [[Astaria]]'s SECRETS if needed.

### What the item has to satisfy:

- It came from Vince's old artificer stock in [[Safilari]]: his shop sold practical enchanted items and gear to practical customers, and he is not a strong wizard. Nothing legendary, nothing combat-grade.
- Small enough to sit wrapped in oilcloth above a ceiling panel for years, and quiet enough that no ambient detection ever flagged it. It only needed to be findable when someone actively swept the block, which is exactly what happened in [[Session 6]].
- Convincing as a source: the strike team found it, logged it, and closed the case. The stronger the item's apparent connection to the actual incident (water pulled from a hundred feet of vegetation, one conjured cloud), the more airtight the closed case reads if [[Howard De Narris]] ever audits it.
- It is now in HUMI evidence, so its craftsmanship is a clock: Neridoran make in a mundane couple's ceiling eventually raises the question of who on that block came from [[Neridora]] (already flagged in [[Session 7 Prep - Todo and Story Suggestions]]).

### Options:

- **The dehumidifier charm.** A palm-sized clay knot that endlessly pulls moisture out of the air around it, the single most ordinary object a swamp-country artificer would stock; every home in Safilari wants one in the walls. This is the airtight option: the HUMI finds a moisture-drawing enchantment a hundred feet from a ring of moisture-drained lawn, and the case closes itself. Under examination it reads as cheap, domestic, and boring, which slows the clock down too.
- **The rain ward.** A braided cord hung under eaves to shunt rain away from a doorway or market stall. Same logic as the dehumidifier (a water-manipulating charm neatly "explains" the incident), but a ward that big implies a maker with real skill, which sharpens the examiner's interest in who built it.
- **The preservation jar.** A stoneware jar that keeps whatever is inside it from rotting, standard fishmonger and pantry stock. The closed-case story writes itself in the report: the old enchantment inverted and fed on the surrounding green to keep doing its job. Bonus texture: whatever fifteen-year-old food Vince left sealed inside is still perfectly fresh, which is exactly the kind of detail that unsettles an evidence clerk.
- **The cooling stone.** A smooth river stone that stays cold, sold by the basket in a country with two brutal summers. Sits in a ceiling for a decade doing nothing but being slightly cold. As a source it needs a lazier strike team ("thermal enchantment destabilized, drained the vegetation"), which is fine if you want the closed case to be visibly sloppy for Howard to find.
- **The prayer lamp (current placeholder).** A brass [[Ulism]] devotional lamp that burns without oil. Emotionally the richest: it's an object of worship from home, and hiding it in a stranger's ceiling as a sacrifice says a lot about what Vince was willing to spend. The cost is that it screams Orochi the loudest: a Ulist lamp puts the HUMI one thought away from "which household here is Ulist," and there's an Ulist family shrine in the Melville apartment.
- **The lost-thing compass.** A pocket compass that points toward what its holder has lost. Travel gear for swamp roads. The danger is the hook: it doesn't stop working in an evidence locker, and whoever picks it up watches the needle swing toward whatever *they've* lost. If you ever want the confiscated decoy to become an active problem, this is the one; a bored examiner following a compass is a plot in a box.
- **The vermin chime.** A little bone-and-tin chime that wards mosquitoes, biting flies, and rats out of a house, the second most ordinary object in a swamp town. The report reads "pest ward misfired and overdrew from local plant life." Comfortably boring under examination, and its materials (swamp bone, reed twine) still whisper Orochi to anyone who knows what they're looking at.
- **The mending needle.** A sewing needle that finishes repairs on its own once started, shop stock for fishermen's nets and work clothes. Quiet, tiny, utterly domestic. Weakest as an incident source (nothing about it touches water or plants), so it leans hardest on the strike team's eagerness to close the case, but it's the hardest option for any later examiner to get worked up about.
- **The guild coin.** A worn brass coin that always finds its way back to its owner's pocket, the kind of trinket the [[City of Zimino]] guild used as a courier's token. The one option with teeth pointed back at Vince's past instead of his present: if a Scholar examiner recognizes guild work, the file stops saying "Orochi immigrant" and starts saying "Orochi *criminal* connections," and if the coin still thinks Vince is its owner, it may eventually try to go home. Highest risk, highest story voltage.

### A note on pairing:

Nothing stops the ruling from being two items: one boring water-adjacent charm that satisfied the strike team (dehumidifier, jar), plus one personal object Vince couldn't bring himself to destroy hidden alongside it (the lamp, the coin). The HUMI logs both, the case still closes, and the second object is the one that matters later.
