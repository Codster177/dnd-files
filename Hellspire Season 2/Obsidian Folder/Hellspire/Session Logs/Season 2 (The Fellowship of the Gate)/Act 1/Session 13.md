## Saga20:
The dragon's defeat left the cavern in ruins, but the party's victory came at a cost: every drop of their drinking water had been expended in the flood that drowned the infernal engine. As the steam cleared and the lava hardened into black glass, Zilton Zalzalm, Na'Hal, and Fel gathered themselves alongside their companions. What lay beyond the newly opened passage was not the frozen mountain they had entered, but a blistering desert waste beneath a gray, searing sky. The search for the unicorn pressed onward, but survival in this unfamiliar place demanded difficult choices: a treacherous climb for snowmelt, a tense infiltration of a strange imp colony, and ultimately a separation from trusted allies as the three heroes took flight toward an ancient temple perched atop a towering plateau.

### Key Events

1. **The Aftermath of the Dragon's Fall**

The cavern still hissed with residual steam as the party caught their breath, the hardened lava beneath their feet still radiating warmth. Zilton Zalzalm placed a firm hand on Skip Leafing's shoulder, the warforged monk expressing gratitude and admiration for the half-elf engineer whose quick thinking had unleashed an entire lake's worth of water upon the hellfire dragon. Skip confessed with humble modesty that he had simply drained a small pond into his bag of holding long ago, a revelation that drew incredulous teasing from Fel, who asked if Skip had truly been carrying pond water all this time. Zilton reassured Skip with humor, pointing out that he himself never drank water at all. When Zilton turned to Na'Hal, his praise was equally earnest, marveling at the banishment that had sealed the dragon's destruction. Na'Hal, still processing the magnitude of what had just occurred, responded with genuine disbelief at his own actions, while Fel stood in quiet confusion, questioning whether the end they had just witnessed was actually some kind of beginning.

2. **The Dragon's Defiled Lair**

Beyond the battle chamber, the party discovered a swampy alcove thick with acid pools and dying vegetation: the original nesting ground of the black dragon before its infernal transformation. Zilton Zalzalm investigated the space, recognizing the creature's nature in the arrangement of its lair. Though a cursory search yielded nothing, Fel's sharper eyes noticed an empty impression in the stone where a hoard should have rested. Gold and bodies had both been collected here once, but the devils who corrupted the dragon had stripped everything of value. Zilton felt a nagging certainty that something had been missed in the rubble of the collapsed chamber, but even with help, the shifting stone revealed nothing intact. What did present itself was a long tunnel, newly cleared by the floodwaters, leading away from the mountain's heart and toward a distant, unfamiliar light. Zilton voiced hopeful optimism that the most grueling part of their journey must surely be behind them, a sentiment that made Fel recoil with superstitious dread.

3. **Into the Burning Waste**

The party emerged from the tunnel expecting frigid alpine air, but instead found themselves shedding their winter garments in confusion. A vast desert stretched before them under a dark gray sky, the sun a pale smear through the haze. The heat was dry and oppressive, utterly unlike the dense humidity of the cave. To the north lay endless dunes and rocky formations, dominated by an enormous plateau that rose like a monument on the horizon. Dot, the mechanical hound, continued to point toward whatever scent it followed, but the party faced an immediate crisis: every waterskin was empty. Exhaustion weighed heavily on everyone, and Solace Forger spoke with weary honesty for the group, admitting that all of them except perhaps Zilton were utterly spent. Fel confessed his own vulnerable fatigue, and Skip expressed practical anxiety about resting properly without water. Zilton noted with ironic frustration that a literal desert lay beyond the mountain while fresh water had surrounded them on the other side.

4. **The Ascent for Survival**

Solace Forger suggested a resourceful alternative: they were still on a mountain, and mountains typically held snow at their peaks. The party began climbing, using ropes and careful teamwork to scale the rocky face. As the sun dipped toward the horizon and the temperature dropped sharply, they finally reached a snowline. The problem of water was solved, but new challenges arose. When Skip eagerly began eating handfuls of snow, Zilton corrected him with genuine concern, warning that such behavior would lead to hypothermia. Na'Hal, however, produced an unexpected solution: drawing his spectral blade, he struck a nearby rock and ignited a campfire of eerie green flame. After a moment's consideration, he used a minor trick to shift the fire's color to a more natural orange, though the memory of that unnatural green light lingered in everyone's minds.

5. **Campfire on the Mountain**

As the party settled around the fire, Skip distributed rations while the sun set over the desert below. Zilton Zalzalm fixed his gaze on the distant plateau, his precise perception cutting through the windy haze. He spotted something others might have missed entirely: a thin pillar topped with flame, and beside it, a cave mouth peering from a sand dune, still miles from The Plateau's base. The discovery meant they were not alone in this wasteland. Around the campfire, conversations drifted through the evening: Solace Forger shared her history of growing up in Forest Guard Village, inheriting her healing vocation, and surviving the hellish invasion that scattered her people. Sabrina Liaro added her own memories of those events. Meanwhile, Fel found himself in a private conversation with Sabrina Liaro, struggling to explain his fragmented memories. Everything was, by his own admission, messed up in his brain: he remembered almost nothing before escaping from the gate, and he did not want to alienate the group with the strangeness of his condition. Sabrina Liaro listened with cautious empathy, acknowledging the complexity of his situation while reminding him that trust among them was essential.

6. **Dreams of Another Life**

That night, as the others slept, Fel dreamed. He found himself in the desert, immobilized in a howling sandstorm, watching a black figure approach through the grit. The figure was Aji Skipplestump, the body's original owner, but different: younger, unscarred, with both eyes intact. Aji Skipplestump did not acknowledge Fel, simply walking past while checking a compass that pointed, unnervingly, to a single fixed destination rather than true north. Fel struggled against the paralysis and managed to follow, trudging through the sand until he caught up just as Aji Skipplestump paused. The moment Fel reached him, the ground gave way, and he tumbled through sand and darkness before landing hard in a dungeon hallway. The dream shifted him to a different section of that hellish prison, far from the cell where Aji Skipplestump was kept. Before he could orient himself or search for answers, consciousness pulled him back to the waking world.

7. Na'Hal **Among the Sand Babies**

In the morning, Na'Hal made a bold decision. Activating his innate ability to turn invisible, he descended alone toward the cave Zilton had spotted. What he found defied all expectations: a colony of nearly a hundred imps living in a crude but organized warren, complete with sleeping quarters, a communal fire, a skinning room where animal hides were fashioned into grotesque face-coverings, and even a designated waste area. These imps were attempting, in their own horrifying way, to mimic civilized society. Ten warlock imps held elevated status, and all of them worshipped a being they called the Immortal: an imp who had somehow lived for twenty years, an incomprehensible lifespan for creatures that normally survived only one or two. Na'Hal, still invisible, observed their butchering room and discovered the corpse of a unicorn, its horn already removed. When he finally revealed himself and was questioned, Na'Hal deflected suspicion with a bizarre claim about coming from different circles, and the imps accepted it. He learned that a warlock named Cross had already taken the horn up to the temple on The Plateau to offer it to the Immortal. Before leaving, Na'Hal found himself in an absurd exchange with an imp named Naz, who showed off his skinworking and, with disturbing pride, his preferred state of undress. Na'Hal extricated himself with bemused surprise that his deception had worked so effortlessly on creatures he had assumed would be too simple to fool.

8. **The Horn's Whereabouts**

Returning to the group, Na'Hal shifted back into his familiar tiefling form and relayed everything: the imp colony, the dead unicorn, the horn's location in the temple above. He described the society with a mix of fascination and horror: their crude imitation of language, their hierarchy under the warlocks and the mysterious Immortal, their earnest but grotesque attempts at personhood. The stag's warning echoed in his memory: when encountering the unicorn, do not attack. The unicorn had already been dead, likely killed by the imps, and the stag's words now seemed to carry a different, more cryptic meaning. Na'Hal also shared his observation that the Immortal was described as larger than the other imps, much like Na'Hal himself, hinting at some connection or shared nature that warranted further investigation.

9. **Parting Ways at the Dunes**

A difficult strategy session followed. The temple on The Plateau was accessible only by flight or an impossible climb: Scarlet possessed winged boots, and Na'Hal could cast a spell to grant flight to three beings. That meant only Zilton Zalzalm, Na'Hal, Fel, and Dot could make the ascent. Na'Hal proposed the plan with determined leadership: the three heroes would fly up while Solace Forger, Sabrina Liaro, Skip Leafing, and Scarlet returned to Forest Guard Village. It was the safest option. Zilton urged them to travel together and promised to send word to Layla Tenama about their return. Skip received heartfelt thanks from both Zilton and Solace, who acknowledged that the engineer's contributions had been vital to their survival. Solace placed a hand on Skip and assured everyone she would lead the group back safely. The farewells were brief but heavy with unspoken concern. Zilton then accepted Na'Hal's flight spell, and the three adventurers, along with the mechanical hound, lifted into the air toward The Plateau.

10. **The Flight to the Temple**

As they flew, Na'Hal shared additional details about the imp colony. He explained how the creatures seemed to be learning languages in a strange, circular fashion, and how they genuinely appeared to be striving for something beyond their base nature, even if their methods were macabre. Fel asked pointed questions about Na'Hal's own nature: how had he become larger than normal imps? What pact had granted him such power? Na'Hal deflected, clearly uncomfortable, and Zilton allowed the subject to drop, noting that larger concerns demanded their attention. They discussed the Immortal: an imp living twenty years was statistically impossible by normal standards, and the warlocks' reverent isolation of this figure suggested something far stranger than a mere long-lived devil. As The Plateau's sheer walls rushed past them, Dot wagged his tail with unbridled joy at the sensation of flight, a small moment of levity before the unknown.

11. **Ancient Steps and Echoes**

The trio landed on a grand stairway carved into The Plateau's face, flanked by massive columns and weathered statues of dragons. The architecture was unmistakably Olympian in its ambition, yet wrong: the stairs descended forward but ended abruptly in empty air, as though the ground had fallen away millennia ago. Zilton Zalzalm studied the stonework and felt a deep resonance in his memory, reaching back six thousand years to an age when his kind had walked this world more openly. This temple was built when The Plateau was closer to the ground, perhaps even connected to a larger structure now lost to time. The history was fragmented in his mind, surfacing only in pieces, but the style was familiar in a way that unsettled him. As they crested the final step and looked into the dark hallway stretching into the heart of the temple, a sound echoed from within: a low, guttural noise, like some enormous creature stirring in its slumber. Whatever the Immortal truly was, it was waiting for them.
